# Vortex

> A 3D visualization of a supermassive black hole accretion flow, inspired by JWST observations of NGC 1275 (Perseus cluster).

## Acknowledgments

## About this reconstruction

This scene illustrates how supermassive black holes are fed: hot gas (10⁸ K) in the cluster condenses into filaments that fall inward, lose angular momentum and join a rotating **circumnuclear disk** (~618 pc, resolved at 10 pc by JWST/NIRSpec). The disk feeds accretion onto the black hole, which launches radio jets that carve cavities in the hot gas — a self-regulating loop.

**Scales are compressed.** The filaments actually span tens of kiloparsecs; the host galaxy and cluster are far larger. This reconstruction compresses scales so the whole phenomenon fits in one view — it is not a literal rendering of the JWST data.

**Artistic view:** light rays are genuinely bent by gravity (Schwarzschild black hole): the disk appears arched above and below the shadow, with the brightness asymmetry of the relativistic Doppler effect. It is an illustration of the central black hole, not a simulation of the paper's data.
